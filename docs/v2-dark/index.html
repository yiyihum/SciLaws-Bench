◆ Scientific law discovery · Preprint 2026

Can LLMs discover scientific laws in real and parallel worlds?

118 problems mined from 381 papers. Each one asked twice — once against the real measurements a research community actually collected, and once inside a calibrated world whose governing law has never been published.

01 / BENCHMARK

Problems a research community is still working on

Not textbook equations. Every problem comes from active, data-driven literature where the published law still leaves room for improvement — and ships with the real dataset it was fitted to.

66 single-group problems use one global law and one parameter set. 52 multi-group problems require a shared functional form that transfers across groups while a few parameters vary — a setting no prior benchmark covers.

02 / SETTINGS

Same problem, two ways to be wrong

Both settings inherit the scientific context, input ranges, validity constraints, and group structure. What changes is where the evidence comes from — and whether a ground truth exists at all.

SciLaws-Real

Fixed records, no ground truth

The measurements are already on the table. A model proposes a closed-form law; published formulas are reference baselines it is free to beat, not targets to recover.

S_N  · numeric fit
Reference-relative on held-out data. The strongest published formula sits at 0.5; a perfect predictor at 1.0.
S_V  · scientific validity
A code-enabled judge runs every item of a frozen, source-grounded rubric, behind a global anti-hacking gate that rejects lookup tables and runaway free parameters.
S_V(t) = a_t · M_t / N_t
SciLaws-Parallel

A queryable world with a hidden law

The same problem, rebuilt as a simulator whose generating mechanism is a newly synthesized variant of the published form. The model starts with zero observations and must decide what to measure.

S_S  · structure recovery
Five levels — 0 unrelated · 0.25 right variables or trends · 0.5 published base form · 0.75 base plus most added terms · 1.0 the complete hidden structure.
Residual calibration
Parameters fitted on the real records, residuals debiased and resampled by k-NN bootstrap, noise scaled so the law stays identifiable within the query budget.
y = f(X) + α · resample(X)
03 / RESULTS

Nine frontier models, one harness

Same ReAct-style agent everywhere: a Python sandbox, up to 30 turns, and a fixed experimentation budget in the Parallel setting. Only the base model changes.

SV, SS and the memorization verdict are LLM-judged. Against majority labels from five domain experts the judges reach Cohen's κ = 0.82, 0.84 and 0.77 — all within the human–human ranges.

04 / FINDINGS

Three things the two settings reveal

Finding 01 · fit vs. validity

A better fit is not a better law

Across 3,616 within-task model pairs with unequal fit, numeric fit and scientific validity point the same way only 54.9% of the time — barely above a coin flip, and reaching 69.5% only when the fit margin exceeds ΔSN ≥ 0.4. On a nuclear-physics task the best-fitting submission invents a resonance peak that does not exist; on the proton form-factor task all nine models submit physically valid laws and seven still lose to the published reference.

Finding 02 · memorization

Memory reproduces published forms; it does not discover new ones

A cold-recall audit splits the tasks into Canon, Mid and Moat. Only 11.9% of problems are recalled by all nine models, while 47.5% are recalled by none — a discovery moat. On Real, models beat the published baseline more often exactly where they cannot recall it (GPT-5.5: 21% → 54% from Canon to Moat). On Parallel, recall lifts recovery of the published base form from 0.59 to 0.85, but recovery of the full hidden law stays flat near 0.08 across every tier, rising only with model capability, 0% → 21%.

Finding 03 · best-of-N

The bottleneck is selection, not generation

Sampling 20 candidate laws per task and letting the model run its own knockout tournament: the oracle gains 4.5 points of fit and 5.3 points of validity from N=5 to N=20, while self-selection captures under 2 points of either. Within the five-candidate groups the selector actually sees, 60% of its winners are Pareto-dominated by another candidate the same model wrote.
