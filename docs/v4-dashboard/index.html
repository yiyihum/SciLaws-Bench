Can LLMs Discover Scientific Laws in Real and Parallel Worlds?

SciLaws-Bench turns 118 open problems from 381 scientific papers into executable discovery tasks — scored on fixed real measurements, and again inside calibrated worlds whose governing law was never published.

How it works

One problem, two settings

Both settings inherit the same scientific context, admissible input ranges, validity constraints, and group structure. What changes is where the evidence comes from — and whether a ground truth exists at all.

SciLaws-Real · 118 tasks

Propose a law from fixed records

The measurements already exist. Published formulas are reference baselines a model may beat, not targets to recover — so there is no single right answer, only a bar to clear.

S_N · numeric fit
Reference-relative on held-out data. Strongest published formula = 0.5, perfect predictor = 1.0.
S_V · scientific validity
A code-enabled judge executes every item of a frozen, source-grounded rubric behind a global anti-hacking gate.
S_V(t) = a_t · M_t / N_t
SciLaws-Parallel · 118 worlds

Run experiments to find a hidden law

The same problem becomes queryable. Its generating mechanism is a synthesized structural variant of the published form — data-consistent, screened for physical plausibility, and absent from the literature.

S_S · structure recovery
0 unrelated · 0.25 right variables · 0.5 published base form · 0.75 base + most added terms · 1.0 complete hidden structure.
Residual-calibrated simulator
Parameters fitted on real records; residuals debiased and k-NN-bootstrap resampled; noise scaled to keep the law identifiable.
y = f(X) + α · resample(X)

What we found

Three results

01 · fit vs. validity

A better fit is not a better law

Across 3,616 within-task model pairs with unequal fit, numeric fit and scientific validity agree only 54.9% of the time, reaching 69.5% only past ΔSN ≥ 0.4. One best-fitting nuclear-physics submission invents a resonance peak that does not exist.

02 · memorization

Memory returns published forms, not new ones

47.5% of problems are cold-recalled by no model; only 11.9% by all nine. Recall lifts recovery of the published base form from 0.59 to 0.85 — but full-law recovery stays flat near 0.08 at every tier, rising only with capability, 0% → 21%.

03 · best-of-N

Selection, not generation, is the bottleneck

From N=5 to N=20 the oracle gains 4.5 points of fit and 5.3 of validity; self-selection under 2 on either. 60% of the selector's winners are Pareto-dominated by a sibling candidate the same model wrote.
